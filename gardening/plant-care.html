3  Herb care on an east-facing balcony

Notes for culinary herbs on a ground-floor, east-facing balcony in Marseille (Provençal south): strong morning sun, shade from midday onwards, hot dry summers, mild winters, and occasional Mistral.

The morning sun is enough for Mediterranean herbs without the scorching west-facing afternoon that burns basil and parsley in July and August. Keep thyme in a separate, drier pot; grow basil, curly parsley, chives, and dill in richer, moister compost.

3.1 Site and watering

  • Orientation: east — water in the evening in midsummer, or in the morning after the first hour of sun. Avoid wetting leaves in full sun.
  • Wind: ground floor is calmer than upper storeys, but still shelter dill and basil from a strong Mistral with the wall or a light screen.
  • Drainage: every pot needs holes and a saucer; empty standing water after watering so roots do not sit in a puddle.
  • Summer rhythm: basil and parsley almost daily in a heatwave; chives every two days; thyme twice a week at most.

3.2 Basil

(basilic)

  • Light / heat: morning sun; afternoon shade in Marseille is an advantage. Bring the pot in if nights drop below about 10 °C.
  • Water: keep the compost evenly moist, never waterlogged. Wilt in the afternoon is a cue to water that evening.
  • Humidity: mist leaves in very dry Mistral weather; good air around the plant still matters (do not crowd).
  • Support: none. See Trimming below.
  • Companions: curly parsley and chives in the same wide pot. Keep thyme out of this mix.

3.2.1 Trimming basil for harvest

Follow the node-and-pair method from Les Sourciers — how to prune basil. Leaves sit in pairs on the stem; at each node, small axillary buds can become new stems if you give them light.

  1. Wait until the main stem has at least two well-formed leaf pairs with visible side buds.
  2. Cut just above a pair of buds, leaving almost no stub (a long stub tends to rot).
  3. Prefer the second pair from the base for the first cut, so the lower buds can thicken.
  4. Each cut yields two new stems; repeat the same cut on those stems as they lengthen. That is how a single spike becomes a bush.
  5. Use the leafy tip you removed. Do not strip single leaves from the lower stem — that is what produces a “bald stick”.

Flowering does not reverse if you keep cutting: the plant has already switched. Stagger a second sowing for a continuous leaf crop. Flowering tops are still edible (oil, drying); the leaves remain edible but thinner.

3.3 Chives

(ciboulette)

  • Light: morning sun; tolerates the east-facing shade later in the day.
  • Water: moderate — let the top centimetre dry, then water.
  • Care: see Trimming below. Divide clumps in spring if the pot is packed.
  • Support: none. A clump in a 15–20 cm pot is enough.
  • Companions: basil and parsley (they like similar moisture).

3.3.1 Trimming chives for harvest

Treat it like a haircut, as in Les Sourciers — how to prune chives: cut the whole tuft, do not pluck a few tubes.

  1. First cut when the leaves are about 15 cm.
  2. In spring and summer, cut every two weeks.
  3. Shear a few centimetres above the soil with scissors or secateurs. Leave that stub so the plant can regrow; cutting flush often yellows the new shoots.
  4. In autumn, one shorter cut helps it into winter. In a Marseille winter you can keep harvesting lightly; if a hard frost is forecast, stop cutting and mulch the crown.
  5. If the clump is dense and stays wet, divide it: lift a wedge with roots and pot it on. Crowded, uncut chives hold moisture and can rot.

Use the cut leaves fresh, freeze in portions, or dry. The purple flowers are edible.

3.4 Thyme

(thym)

  • Light: the most sun-hungry of this list; place at the brightest edge of the balcony.
  • Water: spare. Allow the compost to dry between waterings. Overwatering in a saucer is the usual way to lose thyme here.
  • Substrate: grittier mix (potting compost plus sand or fine gravel). No rich nitrogen feed.
  • Support: none. See Trimming below.
  • Companions: grow alone. It wants drier roots than basil, parsley, and dill.

3.4.1 Trimming thyme for harvest

Thyme lignifies: old brown wood will not resprout.

  1. Snip green tips only, just above a leaf node, as if taking cuttings. Never cut back into bare wood.
  2. After flowering, a light all-over trim of the green growth keeps the mound dense.
  3. Do not “mow” the plant to a stump. If it becomes woody and sparse, start a new plant from a tip cutting rather than hard-pruning.
  4. Harvest little and often through the growing season; a large one-off cut in midsummer can kill a pot-grown plant in heat.

3.5 Dill

(aneth)

  • Light: morning sun; it bolts (runs to flower) fast in Marseille heat, so successive small sowings work better than one large plant.
  • Water: regular, like parsley; do not let the pot bake.
  • Support: stems are brittle — a thin cane or a ring of string once the plant is above about 30 cm.
  • Care: see Trimming below.
  • Companions: own pot. Dill can inhibit some neighbours and grows tall enough to shade basil.

3.5.1 Trimming dill for harvest

Dill is a short-lived annual. You harvest foliage; you cannot turn it into a perennial bush like basil.

  1. Take young leaves by snipping whole side stems, not by stripping the main axis bald.
  2. For a leaf crop, cut before the flower umbel opens — flavour drops and the plant then puts energy into seed.
  3. Leave one plant to flower if you want seeds (pickles) or to feed hoverflies.
  4. In Marseille heat it bolts fast: sow a pinch every two to three weeks rather than relying on one plant.
  5. When a stem is for the vase or the kitchen, cut it near the base; the remaining seedlings in the pot will take over.

3.6 Curly parsley

(persil frisé)

  • Light: morning sun and afternoon shade suit it; it scorches in full south or west sun in July.
  • Water: never let the compost dry out. Deep pot (taproot): 20 cm or more.
  • Humidity: appreciates a light mist in dry wind, like basil.
  • Support: none. See Trimming below.
  • Companions: basil and chives. Slow to germinate — buy a seedling if you want a crop this month.

3.6.1 Trimming curly parsley for harvest

Parsley grows from a central crown. If you cut that, the plant stops.

  1. Always take outer stems, cutting them at the base (soil level), not halfway up the stalk.
  2. Leave the inner, paler shoots — they are next week’s leaves.
  3. Harvest regularly; neglected plants send up a flower stalk (biennial) and then the leaf quality falls.
  4. If a flower stalk appears, cut it out at the base unless you want seed. Start a replacement pot: parsley in a container is not worth keeping once it has bolted in summer heat.
